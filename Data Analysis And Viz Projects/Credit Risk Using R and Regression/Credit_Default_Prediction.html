Dependent variable :

  • default : 1 if the client made a default 0 else

Raw variables to include :

  • senority : (negative) Longer job tenure signals stable income and lower risk of sudden job loss

  • age : (negative) older borrowers are more experienced when managing their money and often have more stable financial situation which lead to lower default risk

Variables to transform :

  • Is_house_owner is equal to 1 if the client own a house
    • Intuition : (negative) Homeowners have a valuable asset and may have already loan money to buy it so I expect lower default.
  • is_married is equal to 1 if the client is married else 0
    • Intuition : (negative) married couple have to income to reimburse a debt and a more stable situation which imply lower default risk.
  • has_fixed_job : 1 if job = “fixed” else 0
    • Intuition : (negative) a full time salary job mean stable income and so less financial situation volatility
  • is_freelance : 1 if job = “freelance” else 0
    • Intuition : (positive) a freelance job is synonym of volatile income and changing financial situation which induce higher default risk
  • is_parttime : part-time job are often mean limited and unstable income
  • reimbursement_cap : expenses / income
    • Intution : (positive) High expenses leave little disposable income to service the loan, so the expenses-to-income ratio should be positively related to default.
  • income_to_total_debt : income / (debt + amount)
    • Intuition : (negative) the higher is the debt compared to income the hardest it will be for the client to reimburse it
  • savings_to_total_debt : savings / (debt + amount)
    • Intuition : (negative) Savings are a liquidity cushion that lets a borrower keep paying during a temporary income shock, so the lower it is compared to debt the higher the higher the risk
  • log(1+income) :
    • Intuition : (negative) Higher income means more capacity to absorb repayments and unexpected shocks, so I expect a negative sign. I use the log because the effect shows diminishing returns.
  • log(1+asset) :
    • Intuition : (negative) same principle than income

To exclude of the analysis :

  • id : identifier of the client

Final regression equation :

This corresponds to the following final equation : \[ \begin{aligned} \text{default}_i =\;& \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{seniority}_i + \beta_2 \text{age}_i \\ &+ \beta_3 \text{Is_house_owner}_i + \beta_4 \text{Is_married}_i \\ &+ \beta_5 \text{has_fixed_job}_i + \beta_6 \text{is_freelance}_i + \beta_7 \text{is_parttime}_i \\ &+ \beta_8 \text{reimbursement_cap}_i + \beta_9 \text{income_to_total_debt}_i \\ &+ \beta_{10} \text{savings_to_total_debt}_i \\ &+ \beta_{11} \ln(1+\text{income}_i) + \beta_{12} \ln(1+\text{assets}_i) + \varepsilon_i \end{aligned} \]

Interpretation :

  • seniority : negative coefficient as expected, significant at 99% level
  • age : positive and significant at 99% level, meaning that the older is the client the higher is the higher is the default risk. May be due to bigger financial pressure due to children for age range 35 - 60 yo and less financial capacities for age range > 60 yo
  • Is_house_owner : negative as expected, significant at 95% level of confidence
  • Is_married : not significant, may be due to high financial pressure for families
  • has_fixed_job : negative as expected, significant at 95% level of confidence
  • is_freelance : not significant, being freelance may induce more volatility but is often related to higher incomes and more flexibility
  • is_parttime : positive as expected, significant at 99% level of confidence reimbursment_cap : not significant, may be due to the fact that expenses changes when people loan money, so the reimbursment_cap ratio is not relevant anymore
  • income_to_total_debt : negative and large meaning it has a big impact on the default capacity, which make sense because the risk of default is related to the value of the debt compared to the ability to reimburse this debt
  • savings_to_total_debt : surprisingly positive, may be due to colinearity with income_to_total_debt
  • log_income and log_asset : both negative and significant at 95% level of confidence

Application of the result to a 1- year loan 10% IR

We will know consider they asked for a 1-year loan with 10% interest rate, considering no cost of borrowing and no other variable costs :

Earning without default : \[ \begin{aligned} Earning \ without \ default = \;& 0.10 * Loan \ amount \end{aligned} \]

to_predict$earning_wo_default <- 0.10 * to_predict$amount

Expected earnings : using the law of total probability \[ \begin{aligned} Expected \ earning = \;& ( 1 - p_{default} ) * Earning \ without \ default \ - \ p_{default} \ * \ Loan \ amount \end{aligned} \]

to_predict$expected_earning <- (1 - to_predict$p_LPM) * to_predict$earning_wo_default - to_predict$p_LPM * to_predict$amount

A loan is accepted if the expected earning is positive

# if loan accepted : Final decision = 1
